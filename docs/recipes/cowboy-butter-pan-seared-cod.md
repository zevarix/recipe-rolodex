## Ingredients

- <span class="recipe-amount" data-base="6" data-unit="portion" data-format="whole">6 portions</span> **cod**, cut from one large fillet into pieces roughly 2–3 inches wide (use enough fish for your guests)
- **Butter**, as needed for searing and basting
- **Olive oil or another cooking oil**, enough to lightly coat the skillet
- **Shallot-and-herb seasoning**, to taste (the fish/chicken/vegetable seasoning from our Costco dinner)
- **Kinder's Cowboy Butter seasoning**, to taste, for the finish
- **Salt and black pepper**, only if needed after tasting the seasoning blends

## Instructions

1. **Portion and dry the fish.** Cut the large cod fillet into serving pieces about 2–3 inches wide. Pat dry so the outside sears rather than steams.
2. **Heat the pan.** Warm a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add a little oil and butter. Work in batches if all the pieces won't fit with space around them.
3. **Sear the first side.** Lay cod in the hot pan and leave it alone for about **3 minutes**, until the underside develops some color.
4. **Flip and butter-baste.** Carefully turn the cod, reduce to medium heat, add a little more butter and a light sprinkle of shallot-and-herb seasoning, then spoon the buttery pan juices over the fish for another couple of minutes.
5. **Finish with Cowboy Butter.** Add a light dusting of Kinder's Cowboy Butter seasoning near the end. Cook until the thickest portion reaches **145°F internally** and flakes easily. Thickness can change cooking time substantially.
6. **Serve warm.** Transfer to plates, spoon over a little pan butter, and cook the remaining portions in fresh batches as needed.

## Kitchen notes

- **It's a dry seasoning, not a prepared sauce.** Kinder's Cowboy Butter and the shallot-and-herb blend add flavor; butter in the pan provides the buttery finish.
- **The exact fish weight wasn't recorded.** The serving control adjusts the number of portions, not a made-up fillet weight. Choose the amount of cod according to appetites and thickness.
- **Avoid scorched butter.** Lower the heat after turning. Add a splash more oil or fresh butter between batches if needed.
- **Check both seasoning labels** for salt and allergens. A light hand at first makes it easier to adjust.
- **Food safety:** Keep raw fish chilled and cook to **145°F** at its thickest point.
